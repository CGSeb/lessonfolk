## Key ideas
1. **AI makes imitation cheap.** A voice can be cloned from a short recording, a face can be
   swapped or invented in a photo or video, and a message can be written in fluent, personal
   language in any tongue. The old tells, such as broken grammar, are no longer reliable. A
   fake made with AI is often called a *deepfake*.
2. **Scammers use this in familiar tricks.** A "relative" calls in distress and needs money
   now. A "boss" asks for an urgent payment. A video call with a familiar face asks you to
   move money or share a code. A fake shop, profile or celebrity endorsement pushes you to
   pay. Deepfakes are also used to harass people, for example with invented intimate images;
   making or sharing them hurts real people and is against the law in a growing number of places.
3. **Look at the situation, not at the pixels.** Glitches in a face or voice are a poor test:
   they are fading, and a good fake has none. The reliable signs are in the request: urgency,
   secrecy ("don't tell anyone"), an unusual way to pay (gift cards, crypto, a new bank
   account), or a request to skip the normal procedure.
4. **A few habits beat most scams.** Pause: urgency is the tool of the scammer. Verify through
   a second channel you already trust, for example by hanging up and calling back on a number
   you know. Agree a family *safe word* that only your relatives would know. In a workplace,
   require a second person to approve payments and changes of bank details.
5. **If it happens, act fast and without shame.** Stop contact, call your bank at once (money
   sent quickly can sometimes be stopped), keep the evidence, report it to your local
   authorities or the platform, and tell people close to you. These scams are run by
   professionals, and anyone can be caught out.

## Teaching notes
- Analogy: a forged signature used to be hard to make and easy to compare. Now anyone can forge
  a voice. So we do what banks did with signatures: confirm through a second, trusted channel.
- Exercise: give the learner a short scenario ("a message from your manager asks you to buy gift
  cards today and keep it quiet") and ask which signs they notice and what they would do first.
- Misconception: "I would recognise my own mother's voice". A cloned voice can fool people
  under stress. The defence is the procedure, not the ear.
- Misconception: "only older people fall for it". Anyone does, especially when tired,
  rushed or emotional.
- Handle the harassment point with care: the learner may know someone affected. Keep it short,
  emphasise that victims are not at fault, and that platforms and authorities have reporting routes.
- Do not give instructions for making deepfakes or detailed attack methods; the goal is defence.
- Do not claim that detection tools are reliable, and avoid statistics on losses: they age quickly.
- Beginners: ideas 3 to 5 are the heart. Experienced learners: discuss organisational controls
  such as approval rules and verification procedures.

## Check your understanding
1. Why is "look for glitches in the video" a weak way to protect yourself from deepfakes?
   Good answer: good fakes have no obvious glitches and the tells are fading, so the situation
   (urgency, secrecy, unusual payment) is a more reliable signal than the image or voice.
2. You get a voice message that sounds like your daughter, asking you to transfer money to a
   new account straight away. What do you do?
   Good answer: pause, hang up and call her back on her usual number or reach her through
   another trusted channel, use the safe word if you have one, and do not send money before
   confirming.
3. Name two rules a workplace can set to make payment scams harder.
   Good answer: for example two people must approve payments, bank-detail changes are checked
   by calling a known number, and urgent requests from "the boss" follow the normal procedure.

## Completion criteria
The learner can describe how AI makes fakes cheap, identify scam signs in a situation, and
give concrete protective habits and the first steps after being caught out (checks 1-3).
