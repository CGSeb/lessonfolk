## Key ideas
1. **AI is software that does tasks we usually associate with human intelligence**:
   recognising images, understanding language, making recommendations, playing games.
   It is not a single thing but a family of techniques.
2. **AI is already everywhere**: spam filters, photo search on your phone, navigation apps
   predicting traffic, streaming recommendations, voice assistants, chat assistants.
3. **Rules vs learning**: classic software follows rules a human wrote ("if the email
   contains X, mark as spam"). Most modern AI *learns* patterns from examples instead.
4. **Today's AI is "narrow"**: each system is good at specific kinds of tasks. It has no
   wishes, feelings or understanding of the world the way people do — even when it sounds
   very human. Science-fiction "general" AI that can do anything a human can is still a
   research goal, and experts disagree on when or whether it will arrive.

## Teaching notes
- Start by asking the learner where they think they have met AI this week; build on their answer.
- Common misconception: AI = robots. Robots are bodies; AI is software (some robots use AI, most AI has no body).
- Common misconception: "AI understands like a person". Keep it neutral and honest: it produces
  impressively useful outputs, but works very differently from a human mind.
- For experienced learners, go fast and ask them for the rules-vs-learning distinction directly.

## Check your understanding
1. In your own words, what is AI?
   Good answer: software that performs tasks needing something like human intelligence; mentions it is a family of techniques or gives an example.
2. Name two AI systems you have used, besides chat assistants.
   Good answer: any two valid examples (recommendations, spam filter, face unlock, translation, maps…).
3. What is the difference between a rule-based program and a program that learns?
   Good answer: rules are written by humans explicitly; learning systems find patterns from examples/data.
4. How is today's AI different from the AI of science-fiction films?
   Good answer: today's AI is narrow, good at specific tasks, with no wishes, feelings or human-like understanding; an AI that can do anything a person can is still a research goal.

## Exercise
No computer needed. Over the next day, notice three moments where AI is working for you
without you asking. Share them at the start of the next lesson.

## Completion criteria
The learner answered questions 1, 3 and 4 correctly (with help if needed) and can give at least one real example.
