## Key ideas
1. **Bring the document into the conversation.** Paste the text, or attach the file if your
   tool allows it. The assistant only knows what is in the conversation, so it cannot answer
   about a document you have not shared. Before sharing, remove anything confidential (covered
   in Using AI Safely and Wisely).
2. **Ask precise questions, and ask where the answer comes from.** "What does this contract
   say about cancelling?" works better than "explain this contract". Ask the assistant to
   quote or point to the passage it used, then read that passage yourself: it may have
   misread it or filled a gap.
3. **Extract information into the shape you need.** Ask for the dates and deadlines in a
   list, the names and roles in a table, the action items with owners. This turns long text
   into something you can act on, but check the result against the source for anything
   important.
4. **AI can help you make sense of a table.** Paste a small table (from a spreadsheet, for
   example) and ask what it shows, what stands out, or how to present it. It is good at
   describing trends and suggesting questions.
5. **Be careful with exact numbers.** Totals, averages and counts can go wrong when the
   assistant predicts text instead of calculating, covered in Prompting Basics. Some tools
   can run calculations; otherwise, check the numbers yourself or do the maths in a
   spreadsheet.

## Teaching notes
- Analogy that works: **a fast reader who has never seen your files**. Hand them the page and
  ask a precise question, and they answer in seconds. But they can skim too fast, so ask
  them to show you the line.
- Demo: paste (or write) a short made-up document together, such as a fictional apartment
  rental notice, and ask two precise questions plus "quote the passage". Then turn it into a
  list of dates.
- Use only made-up or non-sensitive example documents in the lesson; never ask the learner to
  share real confidential files.
- Misconception: "the assistant read the whole document carefully". With long documents it
  can miss or blend parts; idea 2 (show me the passage) is the habit to keep.
- Misconception: "AI is good with numbers because it's a computer". It predicts text; exact
  arithmetic is a known weak spot without a calculation tool (idea 5).
- Stay tool-neutral: say "if your tool allows attaching files", without naming products or
  file limits (they change).
- For learners who work with spreadsheets, spend more time on ideas 4–5: AI for describing
  and planning analysis, the spreadsheet for exact numbers.

## Check your understanding
1. How can you check whether an assistant's answer about a document is right?
   Good answer: ask it to quote or point to the passage it used, then read that passage in the original.
2. Give an example of turning a long document into something easier to use with AI.
   Good answer: any extraction into a useful shape, e.g. a list of dates and deadlines, a table of names and roles, action items with owners, checked against the source.
3. You ask an assistant for the total of a column of 30 numbers. What should you keep in mind?
   Good answer: the total may be wrong because it predicts text rather than calculating (unless it uses a calculation tool), so check it, e.g. in a spreadsheet.

## Exercise
Needs your own chat assistant — free. Take a non-confidential document (a public report, a
product manual, a terms-of-service page, or a long article). Ask two precise questions and
ask for the passages behind the answers, then extract one list or table from it. Check one
answer against the original and tell the tutor whether it held up.

## Completion criteria
The learner answers the checks correctly, in particular the habit of asking for the source
passage and checking exact numbers.
