## Key ideas
1. **Start small: two or three tasks.** A routine sticks when it is simple. Pick the tasks
   from this course that saved you the most time or effort, and use AI for those regularly
   before adding more.
2. **Know where AI does not help you.** Some tasks are faster done yourself, need a personal
   touch, or involve information you should not share. Deciding not to use AI for them is
   part of a good routine.
3. **Set your own checking rules.** Decide in advance what you always check (facts, numbers,
   names, anything sent to others or published) and what you can use as-is (ideas, rough
   drafts for yourself). This turns the safety course into a habit instead of a worry.
4. **Keep what works.** Save your best prompts and templates (lesson 5) and note what went
   wrong once, so you do not repeat it.
5. **Review now and then.** Every few weeks, look at your routine: what saved time, what
   produced errors, what you stopped using. Tools and your work change; your routine should
   too.

## Teaching notes
- This lesson closes the course: link back to each previous lesson briefly and let the
  learner choose their own tasks. Their answers matter more than your explanations here.
- Analogy that works: **a gym routine**. A few exercises you actually do every week beat an
  ambitious plan you drop after a few days; you adjust it as you get stronger.
- Use the learner's notes and exercises from earlier lessons, if available, to suggest which
  tasks fit them.
- Misconception: "the more I use AI, the better". The goal is useful results with the right
  amount of checking, not maximum use.
- Misconception: "once set up, the routine is done". Results, tools and needs change;
  idea 5 keeps the routine honest.
- End on a positive, practical note, and suggest what to explore next: building with AI for
  technical learners, or a review of earlier lessons with "quiz me".

## Check your understanding
1. Name two tasks you plan to use AI for regularly, and one you will keep doing yourself, with a reason.
   Good answer: two concrete tasks from their own life or work where AI helped, and one task they will not hand to AI with a sensible reason (personal touch, sensitive information, faster alone, high stakes).
2. What is one checking rule you will follow, and why?
   Good answer: a clear rule tied to stakes, e.g. always check facts, numbers and names before sending or publishing, because AI can be confidently wrong.
3. Why review your AI routine every few weeks?
   Good answer: to keep what saves time, drop what does not, fix recurring errors, and adapt as tools and work change.

## Exercise
Needs nothing (or a note app) — free. Write your AI routine in five lines: your 2–3 tasks, one
task you keep for yourself, your checking rules, and a date to review it. Share it with the
tutor for feedback.

## Completion criteria
The learner answers the checks correctly and writes a short personal routine with tasks,
checking rules and a review date.
