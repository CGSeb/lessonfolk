## Key ideas
1. **What you type goes somewhere.** A chat assistant usually runs on a company's servers.
   Depending on the tool and its settings, conversations may be stored, reviewed by people
   for safety or quality, or used to improve future models. The rules differ from tool to
   tool and can change over time.
2. **Know your tool's settings.** Most tools have privacy settings or a privacy policy that
   say whether conversations are kept and how they are used. Look once, choose the settings
   you are comfortable with, and remember that work accounts often follow different rules
   from personal ones.
3. **Some things should never go into a chat.** Passwords and security codes, bank or card
   details, ID numbers, and anything that would let someone log in as you. Treat a chat box
   like a message you cannot fully take back.
4. **Be careful with other people's information and with confidential work.** Health
   details, other people's personal data, and your employer's confidential documents or
   client data are not only yours to share. If your workplace has rules about AI tools,
   follow them.
5. **Disguise before you share.** You often do not need the real details to get help.
   Replace names with "Person A", remove addresses and numbers, summarise instead of pasting
   the whole document. The assistant can usually help just as well.

## Teaching notes
- Briefly link to Prompting Basics lesson 4 if the learner took it ("we mentioned this as a
  mistake; here is the full picture"). One sentence.
- Analogy that works: **a postcard**. You would not write your bank PIN on a postcard, even
  to a trusted friend, because others may handle it on the way. Ask: would I be fine with
  this on a postcard?
- Be honest about your own situation as a tutor without inventing details: say that how this
  conversation is handled depends on the tool and settings the learner is using, and invite
  them to check those settings. Do not state specific retention periods or policies.
- Misconception: "deleting the chat deletes everything". Depending on the tool, copies or
  logs may be kept for a while. Check the tool's policy rather than assuming.
- Misconception: "privacy is only about my own data". Other people's data (colleagues,
  clients, family) and company secrets matter just as much (idea 4).
- Keep it practical and calm, not frightening: the goal is a habit (idea 5), not fear of
  using AI.
- Do not give legal advice about data-protection laws; they differ by country.

## Check your understanding
1. Name two kinds of information you should never paste into a chat assistant.
   Good answer: any two of passwords or security codes, bank or card details, ID numbers, other people's personal data, confidential work or client documents, sensitive health information.
2. You want help writing a hard email to a colleague about a conflict. How can you get help without oversharing?
   Good answer: remove or replace names and identifying details, describe the situation in general terms, and only share what is needed for the task.
3. Why should you look at a tool's privacy settings or policy?
   Good answer: because what happens to conversations (storage, human review, use for training) depends on the tool and settings, and differs between tools and between personal and work accounts.

## Exercise
Needs a chat assistant you use (this one works) — free. Open its settings or privacy page and
find out whether conversations are kept and whether they can be used to improve the model.
Then take a real request you would like help with, and rewrite it with sensitive details
removed or replaced. Show the tutor the "before" and "after" (keep the real details private:
describe the "before" in general terms).

## Completion criteria
The learner answers the checks correctly and can rewrite a request to remove sensitive
details while keeping it useful.
